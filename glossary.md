**Abduction** — Inferring a testable case or explanation from an observed result and possible rule (Ch 9; Appendix A).

**Action idea** — A directive best stated as a specific, observable end product (Ch 7).

**Analytical Abduction** — Testing whether a possible case in a known causal structure explains an observed result (Appendix A).

**Answer** — The governing idea that resolves the reader’s Question and tops the pyramid (Ch 3).

**Complication** — The event, change, or tension in the Situation that triggers the Question (Ch 4).

**Deduction / Deductive Grouping** — Reasoning in which a second statement comments on the first, leading to a “therefore” conclusion (Ch 2; Ch 5).

**Degree Order** — Ordering a class’s members by the degree to which they share a characteristic; comparative or importance order (Ch 6).

**Diagnostic Frameworks** — MECE representations of possible causes, converted into testable yes-no questions before data gathering (Ch 9).

**Disturbing Event** — A development that exposes or threatens an Undesired Result (Ch 8).

**End product** — The observable result marking an action’s completion and enabling completeness testing (Ch 7).

**Governing thought** — The top-level idea summarizing the entire document (Ch 1).

**Horizontal logic / The Horizontal Relationship** — The deductive or inductive relationship among same-level points answering one question (Ch 2).

**Idea** — A statement of new thinking that raises a logical question and relates to other ideas (Ch 2).

**Induction / Inductive Grouping** — Reasoning in which similar observations, actions, or conclusions support an inference about their common significance (Ch 2; Ch 5).

**Intellectually blank assertion** — A category or count that groups points without stating their shared meaning or implication (Ch 7).

**Issue Analysis** — Analysis organized around issues requiring explicit yes-or-no answers (Ch 9).

**Key Line** — The principal ideas beneath the governing thought, each starting a major reasoning branch (Ch 1–4).

**Level of abstraction** — An idea’s generality relative to the ideas it summarizes or supports (Ch 1).

**Logical grouping** — Same-kind ideas answering one question and linked by one stateable relationship (Ch 1).

**Logic Trees** — MECE decompositions of possible actions used to generate, assess, and select solutions (Ch 9).

**The Magical Number Seven** — The short-term-memory constraint favoring grouping and lists of roughly four or five points (Ch 1; Ch 6).

**Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive (MECE)** — A division whose parts neither overlap nor omit anything relevant (Ch 6).

**New Question** — The logical question raised by the Answer and answered by the Key Line (Ch 3).

**Opening Scene / Starting Point** — The relevant structure or process in which a problem arose, shown only enough to orient analysis (Ch 8).

**Ordering from the Top Down** — Presenting each summarizing idea before its supporting details at every level (Ch 1).

**Plural-noun test** — Checking whether one precise plural noun, such as “reasons” or “steps,” classifies every point in an inductive group (Ch 1; Ch 5).

**Problem** — The gap between the actual or prospective Undesired Result and the Desired Result (Ch 8).

**Problem Definition Framework** — A map of the Opening Scene, Disturbing Event, R1, R2, and prior solution activity used to identify the governing Question (Ch 8).

**Pyramid Rules** — Each higher idea summarizes those below; grouped ideas are the same kind and follow logical order (Ch 1; Appendix C).

**Pyramid Structure / Pyramid of ideas** — A hierarchy led by one governing thought and supported by progressively detailed, grouped ideas (Ch 1).

**Question** — What the reader wants answered about the Subject, arising from the Complication (Ch 3).

**Question provenance** — The logical path showing why this reader would ask the governing Question given the Situation and Complication (Ch 4).

**R1 (Undesired Result)** — The present or prospective problem, threat, or unrealized opportunity the reader cannot accept (Ch 8).

**R2 (Desired Result)** — The specific end state wanted instead of R1 and used to evaluate solutions (Ch 8).

**Reader’s beginning question** — The pre-existing question making the document relevant and requiring an answer from its top point (Ch 2).

**Recommendation Worksheet** — A Findings–Conclusions–Recommendations table aligning analysis before reader-focused reorganization (Ch 5).

**S-C-Q Structure (Situation–Complication–Question–Answer)** — An introduction establishing context, tension, one Question, and its Answer (Ch 2–4).

**Scientific Abduction** — Inventing an explanatory structure, deriving a necessary consequence, and testing it when no adequate causal structure exists (Appendix A).

**Sequential Analysis** — Problem solving through five questions: whether a problem exists, where and why it exists, and what could and should be done (Ch 8).

**Situation** — Familiar, noncontroversial context establishing the story’s accepted starting point (Ch 3–4).

**Structural Order** — Ordering ideas by the parts or arrangement of a real or conceptual whole divided MECE (Ch 6).

**Summarizing idea / Summary point** — A higher-level statement expressing the meaning, effect, or implication of the complete group beneath it (Ch 1; Ch 7).

**The Bottom-Up Approach** — Building a pyramid by listing points, finding relationships, drawing conclusions, and inferring the Answer and Key Line (Ch 3).

**The Top-Down Approach** — Building a pyramid by identifying Subject, Question, Answer, Situation, Complication, and Key Line (Ch 3).

**The Vertical Relationship** — The question-and-answer relationship in which each lower level answers the logical question raised above it (Ch 2).

**Time Order** — Ordering actions or conclusions by process sequence or the cause-and-effect chain producing them (Ch 6).
